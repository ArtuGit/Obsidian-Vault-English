Relative pronouns connect a noun to extra information about that noun. The most common relative pronouns are *who, which, that, whose,* and *whom*.

---

## Main relative pronouns

| Pronoun | Typical use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| who | people | *The woman **who** called is my aunt.* |
| whom | people as object, formal | *The person **whom** we met was kind.* |
| which | things and animals | *The book **which** I bought is useful.* |
| that | people or things in defining clauses | *The car **that** I want is expensive.* |
| whose | possession | *The student **whose** phone rang apologized.* |

## Defining and non-defining use

Relative pronouns often introduce a [[Relative Clauses|relative clause]].

- **Defining**: gives essential information
  - *The student **who sits near me** is from Brazil.*
- **Non-defining**: gives extra information and uses commas
  - *My teacher, **who studied in Canada**, speaks French.*

## When the pronoun can be omitted

In a defining clause, the pronoun can sometimes be left out when it is the object.

- *The film (that) we watched was excellent.*
- *The man (whom) I called did not answer.*

You cannot omit it when it is the subject.

- *The woman who lives next door is friendly.*

## Common mistakes

- *The person which helped me* -> *The person who helped me*
- *The book who I bought* -> *The book that/which I bought*
