Indefinite pronouns refer to people, things, or amounts in a general way rather than naming a specific person or item.

---

## Common groups

### People, things, and places

| Some- | Any- | No- | Every- |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| someone / somebody | anyone / anybody | no one / nobody | everyone / everybody |
| something | anything | nothing | everything |
| somewhere | anywhere | nowhere | everywhere |

## Typical meanings

- **some-**: usually positive statements or offers
- **any-**: questions, negatives, or unrestricted choice
- **no-**: negative meaning
- **every-**: all members of a group together

### Examples

- *Someone is at the door.*
- *Do you need anything?*
- *Nobody knew the answer.*
- *Everything looks ready.*

## Singular agreement

Most indefinite pronouns take a singular verb.

- *Everyone **is** ready.*
- *Nothing **was** missing.*
- *Somebody **has** called.*

In modern English, people often use *they/them* after singular indefinite pronouns.

- *Everyone should bring **their** notebook.*

## Other indefinite pronouns

- *all*
- *none*
- *many*
- *few*
- *several*
- *one*

## Common mistakes

- *Everyone are here.* -> *Everyone is here.*
- *I don't know nobody.* -> *I don't know anybody.* / *I know nobody.*
