Word order is the usual arrangement of words in a sentence. English depends on word order more than many languages, so changing the order can change the meaning or make the sentence ungrammatical.

---

## Basic pattern

The most common English sentence pattern is **subject + verb + object**.

- **She** reads **books**.
- **My brother** plays **tennis**.
- **They** built **a new bridge**.

English usually does **not** allow a free order like some other languages.

- Correct: *I like this song.*
- Not natural: *Like I this song.*

## Typical positions

### Adverbs of frequency

Adverbs such as *always, often, usually,* and *never* usually go:

- **before** the main verb: *I **often go** by bus.*
- **after** the verb *be*: *She **is always** tired after work.*

### Place and time

Place often comes before time.

- *We met **at the station** **at 6 pm**.*
- *She studied **in the library** **last night**.*

### Adjectives and nouns

Adjectives normally come **before** the noun.

- *a **small blue** bag*
- *an **interesting** lesson*

## Questions and negatives

Questions often use an auxiliary before the subject.

- *Do you live here?*
- *Where does she work?*
- *Has he finished?*

Negatives usually place *not* after the auxiliary.

- *I **do not** understand.*
- *She **is not** ready.*

> [!TIP]
> If a sentence sounds wrong, first check the order of subject, verb, object, and time expressions.

## Common mistakes

- *Always I drink coffee.* -> *I always drink coffee.*
- *He explained me the problem.* -> *He explained the problem to me.*
- *In the garden yesterday we played.* -> *We played in the garden yesterday.*
