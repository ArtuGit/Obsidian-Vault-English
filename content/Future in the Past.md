
“Future in the Past” describes the future **from a past viewpoint**: you look back to a moment in the past, and talk about what was future *at that time*. It’s very common in storytelling and (especially) in indirect/reported speech. Compare with [[Future Simple]] (future from *now*) and [[Past Simple]] (what actually happened).

>[!NOTE]
>“Future in the Past” is more like a **time perspective** than one single tense. English uses several structures (would / was going to / was about to / etc.) depending on meaning.

---

## Core idea

Think of two timelines:
- **Past reference point** (a past moment)
- **Later event** (future relative to that past moment)

Examples:
- Yesterday at 6 PM (past reference point), I knew I **would be** late. *(late happens after 6 PM)*
- In 2019, she said she **was going to** move. *(moving is after the moment of speaking in 2019)*

---

## Main structures (and meaning)

### 1) **would** + base verb (future simple “from the past”)

This is the most common choice when you backshift [[Future Simple]] in reported speech.

| Form | Example | Typical meaning |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| would + base verb | He said he **would call**. | prediction / promise / decision from past |
| wouldn’t + base verb | She said she **wouldn’t go**. | refusal / negative prediction |
| Would + subject + base verb? | **Would** you **help** me? | also used as a polite request (not time-related) |

>[!IMPORTANT]
>In indirect speech, **will → would** is the usual shift:
>- Direct: “I **will** call you.”
>- Indirect: He said he **would** call me.

### 2) **was/were going to** + base verb (past plan/intention)

Often suggests a plan/intention that existed in the past — and **very often** (but not always) that it didn’t happen.

| Form | Example | Typical meaning |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| was/were going to + base verb | I **was going to leave** early. | plan/intention in the past |
| wasn’t/weren’t going to + base verb | They **weren’t going to agree**. | lack of intention |

### 3) **was/were about to** + base verb (immediate future from past)

Used for something that was going to happen **very soon** after the past reference point.

| Form | Example | Typical meaning |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| was/were about to + base verb | The train **was about to leave**. | “just going to” / imminent |

### 4) **was/were to** + base verb (scheduled / expected / formal)

More formal; often appears in news, reports, and narrative style.

| Form | Example | Typical meaning |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| was/were to + base verb | The President **was to visit** France. | scheduled/expected (formal) |

### 5) “Future continuous/perfect” in the past

When you need the *aspect* (continuous/perfect), you can shift it too:

| Meaning | Structure | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Future continuous (past viewpoint) | would be + -ing | She said she **would be waiting** outside. |
| Future perfect (past viewpoint) | would have + past participle | He promised he **would have finished** by Friday. |

---

## Uses

### 1) Reported speech (backshifting)

Used when you report what someone said/thought in the past.

- “I’ll help you.” → He said he **would** help me.
- “We’re going to move.” → They said they **were going to** move.
- “It’s going to rain.” → She thought it **was going to** rain.

See also: [[Going to Future]], [[Future Simple]]

### 2) Storytelling / narrative viewpoint

Writers often “stand” in the past and look forward.

- He didn’t know it then, but that decision **would change** his life.
- I felt nervous. I **was about to** speak in front of everyone.

### 3) Past plans and intentions (especially unrealized ones)

- I **was going to** call you, but I fell asleep.
- We **were going to** meet on Friday, but she canceled.

>[!TIP]
>If you want to strongly suggest the plan **didn’t happen**, “was/were going to” is often the clearest choice.

---

## Time markers / signal words

Common markers when the reference point is in the past:

| Time expressions | Examples |
|------------------|----------|
| the next day / the following day | She said she’d call **the next day**. |
| later / soon | I knew I’d understand **later**. |
| that night / the next week | They said they were leaving **that night**. |

---

## Quick comparison

| Structure | Best for | Example |
|----------|----------|---------|
| would + base verb | reported speech / prediction from past | He said he **would** call. |
| was/were going to | past intention/plan | I **was going to** study medicine. |
| was/were about to | something imminent | She **was about to** cry. |
| was/were to | scheduled/expected (formal) | The meeting **was to** start at 9. |

---

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|------------|-----------|-----|
| He said he **will** call. | He said he **would** call. | Reported speech often shifts *will → would* |
| I **would to** call you. | I **would call** you. | No *to* after *would* |
| We **were going** move. | We **were going to** move. | Need *to* in *going to* |
| She was about to **left**. | She was about to **leave**. | Base verb after *to* |
| He said he would **can** help. | He said he **could** help. | Modals often backshift (can → could) |

>[!WARNING]
>Don’t confuse **would (future in the past)** with **would (conditional/polite)**.
>- Future in the past: He said he **would call**.
>- Conditional: If I had time, I **would call**. (see [[Second Conditional]])

---

## Practice tips

1) **Backshift practice:** rewrite direct speech as reported speech.
- “I will email you tomorrow.” → He said he __________ the next day.
- “We are going to start soon.” → They said they __________ soon.

2) **Choose the best structure:** would / was going to / was about to.
- I picked up my bag. I __________ leave. *(imminent)*
- I __________ buy a new laptop, but I changed my mind. *(intention + didn’t happen)*
- She said she __________ be late. *(reported prediction)*
