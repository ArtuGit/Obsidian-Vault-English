Relative clauses add information about a noun. They are often introduced by [[Relative Pronouns]] such as *who, which, that,* and *whose*.

---

## Defining relative clauses

These clauses give essential information.

- *The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.*
- *The book **that I borrowed** was excellent.*

Without the clause, the meaning is incomplete.

## Non-defining relative clauses

These clauses add extra information and are separated by commas.

- *My car, **which is ten years old**, still runs well.*
- *Sam, **who studied in Leeds**, now works in London.*

In non-defining clauses, *that* is not normally used.

## Omitting the pronoun

You can sometimes omit the pronoun when it is the object.

- *The person (who) I met was very helpful.*
