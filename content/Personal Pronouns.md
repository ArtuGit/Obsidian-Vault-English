
Personal pronouns replace a noun (a person/thing) so you don’t repeat it: *Maria is late* → *She is late.*

They mainly show:
- **person** (1st/2nd/3rd)
- **number** (singular/plural)
- sometimes **gender** (he/she)
- **grammatical role** (subject vs object)

Related notes: [[Parts of Speech]], [[Subject, Predicate, and Object]], [[Possessives]], [[Demonstratives]]

---

## 1) Quick reference (subject vs object)

| Person | Subject | Object |
|---|---|---|
| 1st singular | I | me |
| 2nd singular/plural | you | you |
| 3rd singular (male) | he | him |
| 3rd singular (female) | she | her |
| 3rd singular (thing/animal/idea) | it | it |
| 1st plural | we | us |
| 3rd plural | they | them |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Choose **subject vs object** by the pronoun’s *job* in the sentence, not by what “sounds formal”.
> - **Subject** = does the verb: *She called.*
> - **Object** = receives the action / follows a preposition: *I saw her.* / *with him*

---

## 2) Subject pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)

Use a **subject pronoun** when the pronoun is the **subject**:

- *I* work here.
- *They* are ready.
- *He* doesn’t like coffee.

You also see subject pronouns right before a verb in questions:

- *Do you* understand?
- *Are they* coming?

> [!TIP]
> If you can replace the pronoun with a name as the subject, you need the **subject** form:
> *She* called. → *Maria* called.

---

## 3) Object pronouns (me, you, him, her, it, us, them)

Use an **object pronoun**:

1) as a **direct object** (*whom/what?*):
- I met *him* yesterday.
- She invited *us*.

2) as an **indirect object** (*to/for whom?*):
- Can you send *me* the link?
- They gave *her* a key.

3) after a **preposition**:
- between *you* and *me*
- with *them*
- for *him*

> [!CAUTION]
> A very common mistake is **between you and I**.
> After a preposition (*between*), use the **object** form: *between you and me*.

---

## 4) Pronoun reference (what does it refer to?)

A pronoun should point clearly to a noun (its **antecedent**).

- Clear: *I talked to Anna. She agreed.*
- Unclear: *I talked to Anna about Sarah. She agreed.* (Who?)

Fix unclear reference by repeating the noun or rephrasing:
- *I talked to Anna about Sarah, and Anna agreed.*

> [!NOTE]
> In longer sentences, it’s often safer to repeat the noun once than to use a pronoun that could refer to two different things.

## 5) “It” in real English (not only “a thing”)

### A) Normal reference

- *Where’s my phone?* **It**’s on the table.

### B) “Dummy” / “empty” it (weather, time, distance)

- **It**’s raining.
- **It**’s late.
- **It**’s 10 km to the station.

### C) “Anticipatory” it (real subject comes later)

- **It**’s important **to practice every day**.
- **It** surprised me **that she apologized**.

> [!TIP]
> If a sentence feels heavy at the start, use **It + be + adjective/noun + to… / that…**.
> This links nicely with [[Cleft Sentences — It]].

## 6) Singular “they” (gender-neutral / unknown person)

Use **they/them/their** for one person when:
- you don’t know the gender (*Someone left their umbrella*)
- gender doesn’t matter (*If a student needs help, they can ask*)
- a person prefers *they* pronouns

Grammar agreement is usually **plural**:
- *Someone called.* **They are** waiting outside.

> [!WARNING]
> Don’t mix forms:
> - ✅ *Each student must bring their laptop. They are responsible for it.*
> - ❌ *Each student must bring their laptop. He is responsible for it.* (unless you’ve chosen a consistent generic “he”, which is less common today)

---

## 7) Common patterns and mistakes

### “X and I” vs “X and me”

- **Subject**: *My friend and I* went home.
- **Object**: She invited *my friend and me*.

> [!TIP]
> Quick test: remove the other person.
> - *I went home* ✅ → *My friend and I went home*
> - *She invited me* ✅ → *She invited my friend and me*

### “It’s me” vs “It is I”

In everyday English, **It’s me** is the normal choice.

> [!NOTE]
> Very formal grammar sometimes prefers *It is I*, but it can sound unnatural in modern speech.

Choose the correct pronoun.

1) Sarah and (I / me) are coming.
2) Can you help (we / us) with this?
3) This gift is for Tom and (I / me).
4) Someone forgot (their / his) phone.
