*Must*, *have to*, and *need to* all express necessity, but they are used in slightly different ways.

---

## Main meanings

### Must

Use *must* for strong obligation, rules from the speaker, or logical certainty.

- *You **must** wear a seat belt.*
- *I **must** remember to email her.*
- *He left at 6, so he **must** be home now.*

### Have to

Use *have to* for outside rules, facts, or practical necessity.

- *I **have to** get up early for work.*
- *Students **have to** show their ID.*

### Need to

Use *need to* for something necessary or important.

- *We **need to** talk.*
- *You **need to** back up your files.*

## Negative forms

- *must not / mustn't* = prohibition
- *do not have to* = no obligation
- *do not need to* = no necessity

Compare:

- *You **mustn't** park here.* = it is forbidden
- *You **don't have to** come early.* = it is optional

## Questions

Questions usually use *have to* or *need to* more naturally than *must*.

- *Do we have to book in advance?*
- *Do I need to bring my passport?*
