*Should* and *ought to* are used for advice, expectation, and mild obligation. In everyday English, *should* is much more common.

---

## Advice

- *You **should** get some rest.*
- *You **ought to** apologise.*

Both forms suggest the best thing to do, but they are weaker than *must*.

## Expectation

- *The bus **should** arrive soon.*
- *The package **ought to** be here by Friday.*

## Negative form

- *You **shouldn't** stay up so late.*
- *You **ought not to** ignore the warning.*

*Ought not to* is correct but less common in conversation.

## Common patterns

- advice: *You should see a doctor.*
- criticism: *You should not speak like that.*
- expectation: *This plan should work.*
