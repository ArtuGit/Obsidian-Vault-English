*Can*, *could*, and *be able to* are used for ability, possibility, permission, and requests. They overlap in meaning, but they are not always interchangeable.

---

## Ability

- *I **can** swim.*
- *She **could** read at the age of four.*
- *We will **be able to** finish tomorrow.*

Use *can* for present ability, *could* for general past ability, and *be able to* when you need other tenses.

### Past achievement

For one successful action in the past, English often prefers *was/were able to* or *managed to*.

- *We **were able to** catch the last train.*
- *She **managed to** solve the problem.*

## Permission and requests

- *Can I open the window?*
- *Could you help me for a minute?*

*Could* is usually more polite than *can*.

## Possibility

- *It **can** get cold here in winter.*
- *This road **could** be dangerous at night.*

> [!TIP]
> Use *be able to* when a modal form is not possible, for example after *will*, *have*, or *want to*.

## Common mistakes

- *I will can come tomorrow.* -> *I will be able to come tomorrow.*
- *She could finish the puzzle yesterday* can mean general ability, not one clear achievement.
